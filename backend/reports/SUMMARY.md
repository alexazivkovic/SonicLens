## Key findings

- **Analysed span matters.** The same librosa features computed on the 30 s clip instead of the full track lose 0.069 R² on average; the largest drops are liveness -0.139, valence -0.118, energy -0.066. Echo Nest labels describe full tracks, so part of every clip-based model's error comes from audio it never sees.
- **On identical clips, SonicLens features beat librosa** on 6 of 7 targets (by more than one standard error, ≈0.012), with half as many inputs (257 vs. 518): danceability +0.127, speechiness +0.085, valence +0.079, acousticness +0.033, energy +0.020, instrumentalness +0.018. Tied (within one standard error): liveness.
- **SonicLens on 30 s clips even beats librosa on full tracks** for danceability, speechiness.
- **Most useful descriptor family per target** (largest ablation loss; "none" when every loss is within noise, ≈0.012): energy: rhythm (-0.028), danceability: rhythm (-0.108), valence: rhythm (-0.081), acousticness: timbre (-0.024), instrumentalness: timbre (-0.040), liveness: none, speechiness: rhythm (-0.057). Families overlap in information, so removing one lets the others compensate; the dynamics family (8 inputs) is redundant with the rest for every target.
- **Liveness is barely learnable from a 30 s centre clip** (test R² 0.12). Full-track librosa features reach 0.24, consistent with live-recording cues (applause, crowd noise) sitting at the start and end of tracks.

**DSP tempo check:** SonicLens tempo (30 s clip) vs. Echo Nest tempo (full track): 56.4% within ±4%, 69.5% counting double/half tempo (13103 clips). See `tempo_agreement.png`.
